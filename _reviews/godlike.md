Godlike (godlike.host) is our **#4 rated Minecraft host in 2026** because it does the thing most of this market still refuses to do: it **names the chip**. The Minecraft product is sold on **AMD Ryzen 9 9950X3D** nodes — a 16-core Zen 5 part with 3D V-Cache and a 5.7 GHz boost — which is as close as consumer silicon currently gets to "the right CPU for a Minecraft tick loop." Operated by **Godlike Digital Solutions S.R.L.** out of Brașov, Romania (reg. no. 49011827), the company has been around since 2021 and sells Java, Bedrock, and a long list of other game servers from a Pterodactyl-based panel with a 12-region footprint.

That hardware disclosure is the rating. The rest of the product is a competent mid-market Minecraft host: RAM-tiered Java plans starting at **€6.29/mo (~$6.90) for 2 GB**, one-click CurseForge/Modrinth/FTB installs, a server-splitter, Path.net DDoS mitigation, and 24/7 chat. It is not the cheapest recurring plan in this comparison, the 3-day refund window is short, and the same FAQ that names the 9950X3D also names an **EPYC 9474F** fallback — so you should confirm the chip for the region you actually pick. None of that knocks it off the podium, because the named 9950X3D is a stronger single-thread story than anything [Nodecraft](/reviews/nodecraft/) or [Apex Hosting](/reviews/apex-hosting/) publish on their standard tiers.

This review reflects pricing, plan names, and policy language fetched from Godlike's public pages on 21 September 2026. EUR is the catalogue currency; USD figures use the same ~1.10 FX rate as our other EUR-priced reviews. Frequent flash sales mean the number on the homepage banner is often a promo, not the renewal rate — we score on the published list price.

## Why we rate it 8.4

Four things put Godlike ahead of Nodecraft and the rest of the mid-pack:

1. **It publishes an exact SKU, and the SKU is the right one.** [ServerPrism](/reviews/serverprism/) names a Ryzen 9 *family*. [CloudNord](/reviews/cloudnord/) names a Ryzen 7 7700 at 4.9 GHz. Godlike names a **Ryzen 9 9950X3D**. That is a current-generation Zen 5 chip with 3D V-Cache and a 5.7 GHz boost — extra cache plus high clocks is exactly the combination Minecraft's single-threaded main loop rewards. You can look up Cinebench and real-world TPS numbers for this part before you pay.
2. **The published node spec matches the marketing.** Godlike's hardware materials describe ASRock Rack 2U1G-B650/AQUA nodes with **192 GB of DDR5** (4 × 48 GB UDIMM, non-ECC, DDR5-4800), **dual Samsung 990 PRO NVMe** drives on a **PCIe Gen5** interface, and a **10 Gbps** port with **1 Gbps guaranteed**. Gen5 NVMe and a named consumer 3D V-Cache CPU is more specific than almost anyone else in this comparison.
3. **The panel is a real Pterodactyl fork with extras that matter.** Server splitting, a Discord management bot, three free backups per day, one-click modpack *and* plugin installers, and SFTP are the feature set a 15–40 player community actually uses. NodePanel at Nodecraft is prettier; Godlike's panel is closer to what a technical admin already knows.
4. **Coverage is wide enough for mixed friend groups.** Twelve regions spanning US East/West, Canada, four European countries plus Ukraine, and Singapore / India / Australia is a bigger map than CloudNord's EU-first footprint and comparable to the larger mid-market hosts.

It is **8.4 rather than 9.0** because the recurring 2 GB price is roughly double ServerPrism's, the DDR5 is **non-ECC**, the FAQ still lists an EPYC 9474F alongside the 9950X3D, the refund window is 3 days on a first purchase only, and the storefront leans hard on rotating 75%-off banners. Those are real caveats. They are also the reason this sits behind CloudNord and Nodecraft instead of tying them.

## Performance and hardware

Minecraft's main tick loop is single-threaded. Extra cores help with chunk I/O and the network stack; they do not finish the 50 ms tick. The CPU question that actually matters is "how fast is one core, and how much cache does it have?"

**Ryzen 9 9950X3D** answers that more cleanly than anything else we currently review:

- 16 cores / 32 threads, Zen 5, boost **up to 5.7 GHz**
- **3D V-Cache**, which keeps hot game-state (chunks, entity data, redstone) closer to the core than a standard 9950X
- Consumer desktop silicon on an ASRock Rack B650 platform, which is the same class of high-clock part CloudNord and ServerPrism are pointing at, one generation newer and with the cache stacked on

The published node around that CPU is also unusually specific: **192 GB DDR5-4800** in four UDIMM sticks, **2 × 2 TB Samsung 990 PRO** on **PCIe Gen5**, **10 Gbps** uplink with **1 Gbps guaranteed**, unmetered. Gen5 NVMe is ahead of the Gen4 most of this comparison ships; chunk load and save spikes are the workload that cares.

Two hardware caveats, both material:

- **The RAM is non-ECC.** ServerPrism's 9.0 is built in part on DDR5 ECC at 5600 MHz. Godlike's 192 GB of consumer UDIMM is plenty of capacity and fine for the vast majority of Minecraft worlds; it does not correct single-bit errors the way ECC does on a week-long ATM10 instance. If silent memory corruption is a named fear, ServerPrism still wins that axis.
- **EPYC 9474F is also in the FAQ.** A 48-core Genoa-X frequency-optimised EPYC is a strong server chip and a weaker Minecraft chip than a 9950X3D: fewer GHz on the thread that runs the tick. Godlike's own copy says clocks "range from 3.6 GHz up to 5.7 GHz" depending on CPU. That spread is the difference between "this is our #4" and "this would be a 9." Confirm the SKU for your region in chat before you pay, especially for US West, India, or Australia, where smaller hosts often rack the denser EPYC first.

We are scoring the *disclosed* 9950X3D product Godlike sells as Minecraft hosting, with the EPYC variance counted in the gap down from 9.0. Independent node-by-node TPS numbers are not on the public pages; the rating is a hardware-and-policy rating, same as the rest of this set.

## Features

The Godlike Panel is a themed Pterodactyl build. The useful bits on top of stock Pterodactyl:

- **One-click modpacks** from CurseForge, Modrinth, FTB, Technic, and ATLauncher, plus a separate modded-hosting catalogue with ATM10, ATM9, RLCraft, Better Minecraft, SkyFactory, Pixelmon, Cobblemon, and GTNH pre-configured. Custom packs upload over SFTP.
- **Plugin installer** alongside the modpack installer — Paper/Spigot communities are first-class, not an afterthought.
- **Server splitter:** carve one plan into multiple independent or linked instances (lobby + survival, or a proxy plus game servers). This is the same idea ServerPrism built its rating on, implemented as a panel feature rather than a billing model.
- **Backups:** up to **three free backups per day**, restore from the panel. Retention beyond that is not published as a number.
- **Discord bot:** start/stop/restart, console tail, and player moderation from Discord.
- **Monetization store:** a one-click in-game item store. Irrelevant for a private SMP; relevant for a public community that wants a shop without setting up Tebex from scratch.
- **Personal firewall / IP filtering** on top of network DDoS — useful if you are being targeted at L7 rather than just flooded at L3/L4.
- **DDoS:** Path.net, advertised as **12 Tbps** with L3–L7 filtering, included on every plan.
- **Sub-users** with permission sets for staff.

Modloaders covered in marketing: Vanilla, Paper, Spigot, Purpur, Forge, NeoForge, Fabric. Instant setup is quoted at under two minutes for vanilla and around ten minutes for a heavy pack while the installer pulls files.

What is thinner than Nodecraft: there is no 59-game one-click swap with world preservation across titles (Godlike sells those games as separate products). What is thinner than BisectHosting: the one-click library is "hundreds of packs," not 2,300. For a Minecraft-first buyer those gaps are small.

## Pros and cons

- **Named Ryzen 9 9950X3D** with 3D V-Cache and a 5.7 GHz boost — the most specific, and currently the fastest, Minecraft CPU disclosure in our review set.
- **Published node spec** (192 GB DDR5, Samsung 990 PRO Gen5 NVMe, 10 Gbps/1 Gbps) is more concrete than "Ryzen 9-series" or "high-clock CPUs."
- **12 regions** including Ukraine, Finland, India, and Australia, with a ping checker on the site.
- **Pterodactyl-based panel** with server splitting, Discord bot, plugin *and* modpack installers, SFTP, and three daily backups.
- **24/7 live chat** plus tickets and Discord — wider coverage than Nodecraft or CloudNord.
- **Path.net DDoS** advertised at 12 Tbps, L3–L7, included.
- **Free 2 GB trial** so you can poke the panel before paying.
- **Unlimited player slots** on every paid tier; recommended counts are guidance only.

### Cons

- **List price is mid-market**, not budget. €6.29/mo for 2 GB is roughly double ServerPrism and CloudNord. Flash-sale banners make the gap look smaller than the renewal rate.
- **Non-ECC DDR5-4800** versus ServerPrism's ECC 5600. Capacity is high; error correction is not there.
- **EPYC 9474F is still in the CPU FAQ**, so the 9950X3D is not a contractual guarantee on every node.
- **3-day refund, first purchase only, once per year**, with the EU 14-day right waived on activation. Shorter and narrower than Apex's 7-day window.
- **Budget vs Premium upsell** on the low tiers adds a second price to decode at checkout.
- **Regions, not always cities**, so you still need the ping tool or a chat question for a precise facility.
- **Younger operator** (2021) with a smaller independent review base than Nodecraft, Apex, or Shockbyte.
- **On-site review widget is first-party.** Strong scores, smaller and less independently audited than Trustpilot-scale samples.

## Who is Godlike for?

Godlike fits three buyers in particular.

First, **modpack admins who want a named high-clock chip** and are willing to pay mid-market RAM rates to get it. An 8 GB Unstoppable plan on a 9950X3D is a more honest ATM10 box than a cheaper 8 GB plan on an undisclosed Xeon. Confirm the region is actually on the 9950X3D first.

Second, **communities split across North America and Europe** that want a Netherlands or UK node *and* a US East node in the same panel, with a Discord bot and a splitter for a lobby plus survival.

Third, **people who want a human in live chat at odd hours.** The 24/7 claim plus the on-site review pattern (support mentioned constantly) is the operational pitch; NodePanel at Nodecraft is nicer, Godlike's humans are easier to reach overnight.

It is a worse fit if you want the lowest recurring dollar per GB (Shockbyte, Bisect Budget, ServerPrism), if ECC RAM is a requirement, if you need a contractual 7-day refund, or if you want Nodecraft's 59-game swap on one slot. [ServerPrism](/reviews/serverprism/) remains the value-and-ECC pick; [CloudNord](/reviews/cloudnord/) remains the cheaper disclosed-clock pick for EU players.

## Verdict

Godlike earns **8.4/10** and the fourth slot in our 2026 ranking. The case is concentrated: it publishes a **Ryzen 9 9950X3D** as the Minecraft CPU, wraps it in a Gen5 NVMe node spec you can actually read, and ships a Pterodactyl panel with splitting, backups, and 24/7 chat across 12 regions. That is a stronger hardware disclosure than Apex, BisectHosting, or Shockbyte, and a newer/faster named SKU than CloudNord's 7700. It sits behind ServerPrism, CloudNord, and Nodecraft because the *recurring* 2 GB price is higher, the RAM is non-ECC, the EPYC 9474F still appears in the FAQ, and the refund window is a short first-purchase courtesy. If you care what silicon is running the tick and you can live with a mid-market invoice, Godlike is the host to try after those three.
